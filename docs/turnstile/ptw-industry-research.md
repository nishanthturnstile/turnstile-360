# Permit-to-work workflows across industries

**Research date:** 2026-11-19

HSE's permit-to-work (PTW) guidance describes a recorded authorization for specific people to carry out defined hazardous work at a stated place and time, using precautions identified through risk assessment. Across the industries reviewed, the common workflow is to define the work, confirm the controls, authorize the people doing it, monitor conditions and return the area or equipment to operational control. The steps that decide whether work may start, continue or resume depend on the activity, applicable rules and site procedure. This brief uses UK HSE guidance for operational practice and US federal OSHA standards for examples of regulatory differences. The Turnstile implications are research inferences for discussion. [HSE permit-to-work guidance](https://www.hse.gov.uk/humanfactors/topics/ptw.htm), [HSE HSG250](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg250.pdf).

## Industry workflows

### Petroleum, chemical processing and offshore maintenance

HSE describes displaying issued permits, recording suspended permits, and checking how the work interacts with other planned or ongoing activity. Shared isolations can be linked through an isolation certificate. Issuing and performing authorities communicate outstanding work and plant condition at shift handover. The incoming issuing authority can sign for continuation after the applicable checks. Hand-back establishes what was completed, the condition of the plant and whether operations has accepted control. A suspended permit can retain active isolations. HSE's process isolation guidance also describes proving and monitoring isolations for intrusive work through to safe reinstatement. [HSE HSG250, paragraphs 18–24](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg250.pdf#page=11), [HSE HSG253, figure 3](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg253.pdf#page=21).

US OSHA §1910.119(k) expressly requires a hot-work permit on or near a covered process. The employer records implementation of applicable fire precautions before work, authorized dates and the object being worked on, and retains the permit through completion. Section 1910.119(f)(4) separately requires safe work practices for applicable activities such as opening process equipment or piping and lockout/tagout, covering employees and contractors. [OSHA §1910.119(f), (k)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.119).

### Manufacturing and plant maintenance

The US general welding and cutting rule requires pre-work area inspection and authorization, with written permit as the preferred form under that provision. Where a fire watch is required, it continues for at least 30 minutes after welding or cutting ends. Applicable hazardous-energy control has its own physical and operational requirements. For group lockout/tagout, an authorized employee coordinates protection, each authorized worker applies and removes their personal device, and protection transfers in an orderly way at a personnel or shift change. Outside service employers and the on-site employer exchange procedures. [OSHA §1910.252(a)(2)(iii)–(iv)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.252), [OSHA §1910.147(f)(2)–(4)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.147).

Under the full US general-industry permit-space rule, an entry supervisor authorizes entry, an attendant can determine who is inside, and the permit records test results, controls and rescue arrangements. The supervisor terminates entry and cancels the permit at completion or when a prohibited condition arises in or near the space. Canceled permits are kept for at least one year for program review, including problems encountered. [OSHA §1910.146(e)–(f)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.146).

### Construction and civil works

Under US construction's full permit-space rule, the host employer supplies known space information to the controlling contractor, who relays it to the entry employer and others whose work could create a hazard. The controlling contractor and entry employers coordinate simultaneous entry operations and activities that could create a hazard in the space. The entry employer prepares the permit before entry and reports hazards and the program followed after entry. [OSHA §1926.1203(h)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1203), [OSHA §1926.1205(a)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1205).

The entry supervisor terminates entry when a prohibited condition arises. The supervisor may suspend or cancel the permit when the condition is temporary, leaves the space configuration intact and creates no new hazards within it. Full reassessment is required before reentry. Other prohibited conditions require cancellation. Continuous atmospheric monitoring is the default during entry, with scoped exceptions for periodic monitoring. The permit records how an increase in hazard levels will be detected if ventilation stops. [OSHA §1926.1204(e)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1204), [OSHA §1926.1205(e)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1205), [OSHA §1926.1206(e)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1206).

For excavation near services, HSE advises planning, locating and identifying services, then following a safe excavation method. A written permit may be appropriate for particularly hazardous work. US OSHA §1926.651 gives utility-location and competent-person inspection requirements. Where employee exposure is reasonably anticipated, inspections occur before work, as needed through the shift and after a rainstorm or other event that increases hazards. The permit workflow can therefore carry utility-marking evidence and react to a fresh inspection trigger where the site uses a permit. [HSE HSG47](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg47.pdf), [OSHA §1926.651(b), (k)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.651).

### Electrical transmission, distribution and high-voltage maintenance

HSE's electrical permit is a declaration that the precise equipment and work boundary have been made safe for work on isolated equipment. The designated competent issuer documents isolation and applicable earthing and works with the recipient to confirm the details. A change in work scope calls for cancellation and a new permit. Hand-back includes withdrawal of people, tools and any additional earths and an indication of equipment fitness for service. HSE generally favors cancellation and fresh issue when work has to pause. [HSE HSG85, paragraphs 69–83](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg85.pdf#page=24).

US transmission and distribution rules establish a clearance controlled by the designated employee in charge and, where applicable, a system operator. Multiple crews coordinate under a common clearance or follow the separate-crew provisions. Before reenergization, every crew must release its clearance, employees must be clear, and protective grounds and applicable tags must be removed. A transfer of the person in charge requires notice to the crew and system operator. [OSHA §1910.269(m)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.269).

### Shipbuilding and ship repair

Hot work in specified vessel spaces, at certain boundaries or on connected equipment can require testing and a Marine Chemist or US Coast Guard authorized person's certificate. Other specified spaces can be tested by a competent person. The external certificate is posted near the work and kept for at least three months after completion. Conditions continue to be checked after issue. When a competent person finds a certified atmosphere has fallen out of compliance, work stops until an authorized certifier retests and issues a new certificate. [OSHA §§1915.14–1915.15](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1915/1915.14), [Maintenance of safe conditions](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1915/1915.15).

### Grain handling and storage

US requirements for covered grain facilities provide specific hot-work permit exceptions, including in authorized welding shops and when the authorizing employer or representative is present while work is performed. For covered bin, silo or tank entry, a permit is required under the grain rule unless that person stays for the entire operation. The entry controls include isolation of equipment that endangers entrants, applicable atmospheric testing, an external observer and suitable rescue equipment. Required hot-work and entry permits under this section are kept through completion. [OSHA §1910.272(f)–(g)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.272).

| Availability at the worksite                   | Make the current permit readable at the job or point of entry. HSE's electronic guidance calls for controlled signatures, a site visit before issue, communication of changes, paper display capability and an outage fallback. A Turnstile procedure for outages can identify who controls the paper record and reconciles it when service returns. [HSE HSG250, paragraph 51](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/priced/hsg250.pdf#page=24). |
| Retention by record type                       | Use policy tied to the applicable record and retention trigger. The examples include canceled confined-space entry permits held for at least a year, covered-process hot-work permits held through completion, and external shipyard hot-work certificates held for at least three months from completion.                                                                                                                               |

Three workflow comparisons can validate the design against actual site procedures. First, simulate a prohibited condition in a general-industry confined space and a temporary qualifying condition in a construction space. The first permit is canceled and the second can be suspended for documented reassessment. Second, close one utility crew's work while another crew holds a clearance. The equipment remains unavailable for reenergization. Third, record an out-of-compliance atmosphere in a certified shipyard compartment. The existing certificate becomes ineligible for resumption and the record points to the replacement once a qualified certifier issues it.
